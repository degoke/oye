## 1. Ask a question

Enter a topic, goal or question, such as "Teach me photosynthesis in a few sentences." Choose a learning language and select **Start learning**. **Speak your request** lets you record or upload audio, review the recognized words, and confirm them before starting.

![The learning home with a photosynthesis request](assets/walkthrough/01-request.jpg)

## 2. Find a starting point

A few questions help shape the lesson. Answer what you know, choose **I don't know yet**, or skip and start with the basics. Skipping does not mark a concept as understood.

![A starting question about light in photosynthesis](assets/walkthrough/02-starting-point.jpg)

## 3. Read a sourced lesson

The lesson includes short explanations, a visual summary, practice and source references. Here the key distinction is between materials and energy: plants combine carbon dioxide and water; light supplies energy.

![The photosynthesis lesson and its source references](assets/walkthrough/03-lesson.jpg)

## 4. Watch the video explainer

Choose **Open video** in the lesson's **Watch** section. òye. turns the sourced lesson into a short visual explainer with narration and captions, so you can watch and listen as well as read.

The compound-interest example below shows the explainer playing at 0:51. The video, captions and playback controls are visible, with downloads and the full transcript underneath.

![The video explainer playing in the app, with captions, timeline, sound controls, video and caption downloads, and transcript](assets/walkthrough/12-video.png)

Pause or replay the explanation as needed. In low-data mode, the video loads when you choose to open or download it.


## 5. Explain it back

In **Explain it in your words**, give your own explanation. You can type or speak. This example deliberately includes a misconception: "Light itself becomes plant mass."

![An independent explanation in Explain Back](assets/walkthrough/04-explain-back.jpg)

## 6. Answer a follow-up

òye. asks about the learner's meaning before correcting it. The question below asks for the roles of sunlight, carbon dioxide and water. The explanation and follow-up remain saved together.

![A follow-up question and answer](assets/walkthrough/05-follow-up.jpg)

## 7. See feedback grounded in your words

Feedback identifies what the answer shows and quotes the words supporting the finding. Here, the learner identifies products but denies that carbon dioxide and water supply materials. The concept needs practice.

![Feedback identifying the misconception](assets/walkthrough/06-feedback.jpg)

## 8. Repair the misconception

The repair targets that gap. It separates material inputs from the energy source, then asks the learner to apply the distinction to a tomato plant under a grow lamp.

![The targeted repair and application](assets/walkthrough/07-repair.jpg)

## 9. Explain again and try a fresh example

The learner explains the idea again. The new independent explanation correctly separates carbon dioxide and water from light energy.

![An improved independent explanation](assets/walkthrough/08-improved-explanation.jpg)

A fresh question changes the conditions: sunlight is available during one interval, and the plant is in complete darkness during another. The answer applies the distinction to both. Understanding becomes **Demonstrated** when the independent explanation covers the essential ideas and the fresh application is correct. Earlier attempts stay saved.

![The correct fresh application and demonstrated understanding](assets/walkthrough/09-understanding.jpg)

## 10. Choose your language

Preferences let you choose English, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo, reading support, preferred format and low-data mode. The interface and learning content follow the selected language.

![Learning preferences in Yoruba](assets/walkthrough/10-language.png)

The question below is read aloud in Yoruba. Controls include playback speed, download and a transcript.

![A Yoruba question with its audio player](assets/walkthrough/11-spoken-question.jpg)

The explainer can teach in the learner's selected language. Here, the Yoruba photosynthesis video explains how plants use carbon dioxide and water to produce carbohydrates and oxygen, with sunlight supplying energy.

## 12. Save text for later

Choose **Save text offline** while connected. Saved text includes source references and opens later from **Saved** when the connection drops.

![A saved lesson open for offline reading](assets/walkthrough/13-offline.png)

## Try it yourself

Follow [Getting started](getting-started.md), configure your key, and use photosynthesis as your first request. Generated questions vary, so answer the scenario shown in your app. The tutor helps with the current lesson, and **Progress** keeps the evidence behind your learning state.
